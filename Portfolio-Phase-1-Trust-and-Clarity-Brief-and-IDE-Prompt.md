## 1. Objective

Improve the credibility and clarity of the portfolio without redesigning it or removing its distinctive systems-thinking identity.

The current site is visually differentiated and demonstrates strong product thinking, but parts of its language communicate a level of enterprise deployment, technical maturity, client adoption, automation, and measured impact that is not consistently supported by available evidence.

Phase 1 must:

1. Reconcile professional-experience claims.
2. Replace absolute or inflated positioning language.
3. Distinguish live products, prototypes, internal templates, in-development projects, and roadmap concepts.
4. Separate implemented functionality from sample data and planned capability.
5. Remove or qualify testimonials, customer claims, transaction figures, savings, integrations, compliance claims, and performance outcomes that lack evidence.
6. Preserve the premium visual identity and technical depth.

Guiding principle:

> Show what exists. Explain what it solves. Label what comes next.

## 4. Approved Project Status System

Use only the following statuses across project cards, filters, case studies, metadata, and structured data.

### Live Product

Use when a project is publicly accessible and performs its stated core function with real persistence or a clearly functional operating workflow.

### Active Prototype

Use when the product demonstrates a functioning workflow but still relies on sample data, local persistence, incomplete integrations, or limited validation.

### Internal Template

Use for reporting tools, operational templates, or internal-use artifacts that are not shared production platforms.

### In Development

Use when meaningful functionality remains under active construction or is not ready for dependable use.

### Concept / Roadmap

Use when the work is primarily a design exploration, simulation, architecture proposal, or planned capability.

## 5. Global Claims Rules

Every visible claim must belong to one of these categories:

### Implemented

Functionality observable in the current product or source code.

### Sample / Demo

Illustrative data or simulated interaction used to explain behavior.

### Design Objective

An intended improvement, such as reducing handoffs, errors, or coordination effort, that has not yet been measured.

### Roadmap

Planned functionality that is not yet implemented.

### Verified Outcome

A result supported by documented baseline, measurement method, relevant period, and approval to publish.

Never present Sample, Design Objective, or Roadmap items as Verified Outcomes.

### Required prototype disclosure

Use where applicable:

> Interface data shown is sample data used to demonstrate product behavior. It does not represent audited customer activity, transaction volume, financial performance, or measured operational impact.

### Required confidentiality disclosure

Use where applicable:

> Screens shown use anonymized or sample data. Personal information, company figures, and confidential operational details are not disclosed.

## 6. Claims to Remove or Audit Immediately

### Homepage and hero

- `20+ years` — replace with `15+ years` unless verified from CV dates.
- `Full-Stack Engineer` — replace with `AI-Assisted Product Builder`.
- `100% Execution Fidelity` — remove.
- `Live systems, export platforms, community software, and SaaS dashboards` — replace with language covering live products, prototypes, and internal tools.
- `Engineered custom B2B trade pipelines` — soften if integrations and transactions are not live.
- `Customs documentation and escrow flows` — qualify as workflow concepts or prototypes where appropriate.
- `API sync pipelines` — only list integrations actually implemented.

### Architecture playground and blueprints

The current architecture artifacts include infrastructure and integration details that may look production-real, including internal database endpoints, Redis clusters, replicas, webhooks, EFS volumes, and service images.

Required action:

- Verify every displayed configuration against the actual project.
- If conceptual, label it clearly as `Conceptual Architecture Blueprint`.
- Remove realistic-looking secrets, endpoints, internal hostnames, certificates, or production infrastructure references.
- Do not call a conceptual simulation `live architecture activity`.
- Replace `real-time execution` with `interactive simulation` where appropriate.
- Do not imply deployed microservices when the product is a monolithic prototype or static interface.

### Services and FAQ

Audit or qualify:

- Fractional CTO leadership
- Enterprise and high-complexity project capability
- Multi-tenant architecture
- High-volume data workflows
- Escrow payment engines
- Stripe/payment gateway implementation
- Automated billing and disbursement
- Customs compliance
- Automated monitoring
- Enterprise security
- Sub-second performance claims
- IP/source-code transfer policies not formalized in a contract

Services can mention these as potential scoped requirements only when they are evaluated per project and delivered with appropriate specialists or verified integrations.

### Scope estimator

- Label all prices and timelines as preliminary estimates.
- Do not present an automatically generated scope as an official proposal.
- Remove default assumptions of Stripe, escrow, customs, multi-tenancy, DevOps, or enterprise security.
- Add a disclaimer that final scope depends on discovery, data, integrations, and risk.
- Keep currency presentation consistent with the selected currency.

### Writings

Audit immediately:

- `up to 40% lower shipping overhead`
- Claims that a system was `engineered` or `built` when the article discusses a concept
- Escrow-secured trade flows not implemented
- QR gate control or automated dues claims not present in the current product
- Production outcomes derived from demo interfaces

Change article wording from outcome claims to lessons, design decisions, prototypes, or operating hypotheses unless evidence exists.

## 14. Acceptance Criteria

Phase 1 is complete when:

- Homepage uses the approved core positioning.
- Public industrial experience is consistently stated as 15+ years unless verified otherwise.
- Full-Stack Engineer and 100% Execution Fidelity are removed from the homepage.
- Selected Work acknowledges live products, prototypes, and internal tools.
- All six project statuses use the standard status system.
- Sample data is not presented as customer or business performance.
- Roadmap capability is not presented as implemented functionality.
- Conceptual architecture is labeled clearly.
- Production-looking but fictional configuration details are removed or made unmistakably conceptual.
- Testimonials without verification and permission are removed or replaced.
- Unsupported savings, adoption, transaction, compliance, integration, security, and automation claims are removed or qualified.
- Service copy does not overpromise regulated or high-complexity delivery.
- Scope-estimator outputs are labeled preliminary.
- Biography explains the authentic transition from operations to digital-product building.
- Revised copy works at desktop and mobile sizes.
- Existing checks and production build pass, or pre-existing failures are documented.
- No unrelated design or feature changes are introduced.

## 15. Evidence Register for Future Reintroduction

Maintain a simple evidence register before reintroducing stronger claims.

For each claim record:

- Exact claim
- Project
- Evidence source
- Measurement period
- Baseline and result
- Method
- Owner or approver
- Permission to publish
- Date verified
- Expiry/review date

Claims without a completed evidence record remain excluded, anonymized, or labeled as objectives/roadmap.
